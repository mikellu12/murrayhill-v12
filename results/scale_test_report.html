What the test says

  • Seven anchors do fix the gap. 4 appears on every field — 45 of 64 for walkable_ground, where "a standard sidewalk with room for two abreast" is evidently the common case. P(4) rises everywhere.
  • And they cost accuracy on two of the three checkable fields. sky_openness collapses from +0.363 to −0.034 — no relationship left. The seven rungs I wrote for sky pull the model toward judging canyon shape rather than how much sky is visible. That is the same length effect that broke the twelve-field schema, in miniature.
  • Reading the distribution gets the resolution without the cost. On unchanged two-anchor prompts, expected value gives 64 distinct values instead of 5, no hole at 4, and a better correlation than the argmax on two of three fields. It is also cheaper — one forward pass, no generation.
  • Expected value always narrows the range, and that is arithmetic rather than loss: an expected value only reaches 1.00 if the model is certain, and it rarely is. What matters is whether it orders the streets correctly, which the correlations answer.

Is a low-confidence field just noise?

facade_variation puts only 0.226 of its probability on the digit it emits — barely above the 0.143 of pure ignorance. That means the model is unsure about each image. It does not follow that the field measures nothing, and two tests separate those, neither needing a pixel counterpart.

Shuffling the same values into random spatial order gives Moran's I of −0.003 ± 0.011, so facade_variation's +0.478 sits some forty standard deviations clear of chance.
